Architectural DNA

Gothic Character

Steep spires, exposed ribs, and dark timber — Gothic-Modern architecture is our clearest reference point for structure made visible. The same principle that lets a cathedral roof carry its own weight in stone is what lets our machines carry sensors, wiring, and control systems without hiding the engineering underneath.

We borrow its verticality and its honesty about load-bearing form: nothing is decorative that isn't also structural. That discipline shows up in how we route cable trays, mount sensor arrays, and frame control cabinets on site.

Verticality

Tall, narrow silhouettes that draw the eye upward — echoed in mast-mounted sensor towers and control enclosures.

Exposed Structure

Ribs, trusses, and joints left visible rather than hidden — the same logic behind our unshielded wiring runs and modular chassis.

Dark Materiality

Blackened timber and iron inform our steel and iron color palette across hardware and interface alike.
