Greek Architecture

Greek architecture is built on ratio, symmetry, and load carried through proportion rather than mass — the column doing exactly the work it needs to and no more. That same discipline governs how we choose a joist spacing, set out pedestals, or space railing posts.

Where Gothic-Modern gives us verticality and exposed structure, Classical gives us restraint: clean lines, calculated proportion, and nothing added without purpose.

Proportion

Every load-bearing element sized to its function — the same logic behind our joist sections and pedestal spacing.

Symmetry

Balanced forms that read as stable at a glance — the same principle behind how we lay out distribution boards and stair flights.

Restraint

Clean stone surfaces with no ornament that isn't structural — echoed in our plain palette of timber, steel, and glass.
