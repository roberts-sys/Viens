Field Services

Terraces

A terrace is the one part of a house that stays outside all year. So we build it as a structure rather than a surface — starting with the footings, the fall, and the airflow under the boards, not with a material sample.

Every project starts with a site visit: the ground, the drainage, the height of the plinth, and where the day actually casts shade. The choice of material comes after that — once it's clear what the structure has to carry.

What we build

Materials and solutions

Photo: timber decking grain

Timber terraces

Thermally treated wood, larch, or oak — with concealed fixings, so the surface stays clean and no screw heads rust through it.

Photo: composite board close-up

Composite terraces

Resistant to damp, rot, and UV. The practical choice where a terrace won't get re-oiled every season.

Photo: substructure before decking

Substructure and footings

Adjustable pedestals, a ventilated void, and a fall that sheds water — the part nobody sees afterwards, and the part that decides how long it lasts.

Photo: railings and evening lighting

Railings, stairs, and light

Steel or timber railings, stairs made to measure, and built-in LED lighting — we run the wiring ourselves.

How it goes

From site visit to handover

01

Site visit

Measurements, ground conditions, drainage, and which way the sun falls. Free, with no obligation to continue.

02

Drawing and quote

Layout, materials, and the structural solution — with a fixed price and a fixed timeline.

03

Build

Footings, structure, decking, and finish. The site gets cleared at the end of every day.

04

Handover and care

We walk the finished job together and leave a short note on how to look after it each season.
